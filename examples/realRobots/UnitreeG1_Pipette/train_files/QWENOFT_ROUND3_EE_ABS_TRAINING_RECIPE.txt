0. SUMMARY IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QwenOFT (Qwen3-VL-4B + a 65.6 M-parameter MLP head, 4,503.4 M parameters total,
100% trainable) is fine-tuned on 249 teleop episodes of the G1 pipette-tip task.
The policy sees three 256x256 camera views and one frame per view - it is
memoryless - and emits a 30-row chunk of ABSOLUTE right-hand Cartesian positions
(x, y, z in metres, pelvis frame) at 60 Hz. No proprioceptive state is fed to the
model in this arm. 15% of episodes are held out and scored every 500 steps
against a "freeze the hand at the chunk's first row" baseline. This round exists
because the round-2 head, which predicted PER-STEP DELTAS, collapsed to exact
zeros on the real robot.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THIS ROUND EXISTS - THE ROUND-2 POST-MORTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Round 2 trained the same model on `action.right_ee_dxyz`: the per-step change in
hand position, FK(cmd[t+1]) - FK(cmd[t]) at 60 Hz. Both A/B arms beat their
offline baseline, but on the real robot both STALLED at the same place: with the
pipette hovering directly above the tip, the arm stopped and never resumed.

Flight-recorder evidence (host path; the container's --home bind):
  /tmp/wbc_home/.local/state/policy_bridge/flight/<ts>_<profile>/events.jsonl.gz
  "rec":"chunk" records carry step_mean / step_max / net of the CONVERTED chunk.
  Session 20260912_1917xx (state arm): the zero-chunk fraction climbed
  29% -> 50% -> 85% -> 95% and hit 100% at t~210 s, then held 100% for 6+
  minutes. All 30 rows byte-identical, i.e. |dxyz| < 0.01 mm per step - two
  orders of magnitude below the 0.12 mm median of a real training step.

Two causes, and they compound:

  (a) ZERO-INFLATED LABELS - measured, not estimated.
      The teleop command stream runs at ~50 Hz while the recorder runs at 60 Hz,
      so whenever two consecutive recorded frames share a command the difference
      is EXACTLY zero - bit-for-bit, and always as a WHOLE ROW (the exact-zero
      fraction is identical for x, y and z, because it is the commanded joint
      vector that did not change). Measured over the parquet:

          exact-zero delta rows   39.688%  train   (121,799 / 306,891)
                                  40.873%  eval    ( 22,936 /  56,115)
                                  39.871%  all 293 (144,735 / 363,006)

          |d| over ALL rows       median 0.1129 mm, mean 0.1263 mm,
                                  q01 = q05 = q25 = 0.000 mm (the zero mass
                                  swallows the entire lower quartile),
                                  q95 0.3750, q99 0.6939, max 30.297 mm
          |d| EXCLUDING zeros     q01 0.0171, median 0.1199, q99 0.7598 mm
                                  (n = 185,092)

      Zero-run structure: 43,574 runs, 89.66% of length 1, 6.99% of length >= 3,
      longest 691 frames (11.5 s). Per-episode zero fraction ranges 23.3% to
      67.0%. Two distinct regimes are visible - interleaved single-frame zeros
      (the 50/60 Hz beat) and long deliberate holds - and a memoryless policy
      cannot tell them apart from one frame.

      (The data_config docstring says "34-45%"; the measured dataset-wide figure
      is 39.9% and the per-episode spread is more than twice as wide as that
      band. Quote 39.7% / 39.9%, not the band.)

      An MSE/L1 head learns the conditional statistic of the label given the
      observation; at the hover-above-tip scene, where the operator's alignment
      pauses concentrate, that statistic is pulled onto the zero spike. The model
      is not "moving too little" - its optimal answer under the training
      distribution IS zero.

  (b) A MEMORYLESS POLICY IN CLOSED LOOP MAKES ZERO AN ABSORBING STATE.
      Output zero -> the integrated command does not change -> the robot does not
      move -> the next observation is pixel-identical -> output zero again. There
      is no clock and no history that can break the cycle.

The control that proves (a) is scene-specific rather than static-input-driven:
session 20260912_1902xx ran with the robot frozen at the EPISODE START pose (a
serving bug meant no command was ever published), and there the model emitted 0%
zero chunks for five straight minutes - it kept asking to descend. Staring at a
still image does not by itself produce zeros; staring at THIS still image does.

HOW GOOD WAS ROUND 2, REALLY? Its baseline predicted exactly zero, and 40.9% of
eval-split delta labels ARE exactly zero. "Hold still" was therefore not a naive
competitor - it was literally correct on two of every five holdout steps. Read in
that light, the best arm's mse_ratio of 0.747 says the policy was only 25% better
than a predictor that is exactly right 41% of the time and exactly wrong the
rest. That is the single strongest argument for changing the label space.

Fix: make the label space one in which "do nothing" has no shared special value.
With absolute targets, a demonstration pause labels "hold THIS pose" - a
scene-dependent continuous value. No probability spike is shared across the
dataset, so the collapse cannot be expressed.

Expected ordering of action spaces, unchanged from the earlier analysis:
  absolute  >=  chunk-relative (cumulative)  >  per-step delta
Round 3 tests the top of that ordering. No dataset rebuild was needed: the
absolute column was written alongside the deltas when the -ee dataset was built.

4. STATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
include_state    false          <- THIS is the real switch
state_dim        0              <- inert: nothing in QwenOFT reads it; it is
                                  documentation of intent

This arm feeds the model NO proprioception. The mechanism: QwenOFT prepends
discretised state tokens only `if "state" in examples[0]`, and with
include_state false the loader never packs the key, so the instruction is never
augmented.

The DataConfig still DECLARES the 32-dim state block it inherits (left_leg 6 +
right_leg 6 + waist 3 + left_arm 7 + right_arm 7 + right_ee_xyz 3, in that
concatenation order) so a with-state twin can reuse the config and so the
serve-time key contract stays discoverable.

Rationale for going no-state: in round 2 the no-state arm scored BETTER (final
ratio 0.7469 vs 0.8081), the with-state arm actually OVERFIT (section 11), and
both stalled identically on the robot - so state was not what was broken. One arm
is enough to test the action-space hypothesis.

5. VIEW PIPELINE AND AUGMENTATION  (inherited unchanged from round 2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
video_keys       video.rgb, video.wrist_left, video.wrist_right - consumed
                 POSITIONALLY in this order
Working size     256 x 256 for all three views (round 1 had ego 256 + wrists 448)
Constants        VIEW_SIZE 256, PRE_CROP 270, CROP_FRAC 0.95, ROT_DEG 5.0, with a
                 class-body `assert int(270*0.95) == 256` that fires at import.

18 transforms in one ComposedModalityTransform: 12 per-view + 6 shared.

PER VIEW (4 transforms x 3 views, each scoped to one key):
   VideoToTensor
   VideoResize            270 x 270, bilinear
   VideoRandomRotation    +/- 5 deg, bilinear             [train only]
   VideoCrop              scale 0.95 -> 256 x 256         [RandomCrop train,
                                                           CenterCrop eval]
SHARED (one draw for all three views - one scene, one sensor):
   VideoColorJitter       brightness 0.3, contrast 0.4, saturation 0.5, hue 0.08
   VideoColorTemperature  strength 0.15
   VideoToNumpy
   StateActionToTensor    (state + action keys)
   StateActionTransform   (state keys, all q99)
   StateActionTransform   (action keys, q99)

Per-view vs shared is a real mechanism, not a convention: one random draw happens
per transform CALL. Geometric transforms have apply_to=[one key], so three calls
= three independent draws (independent camera-pose jitter). Colour transforms
have apply_to=[all three], so the views are concatenated and ONE draw is shared.

Three details worth knowing:

  * THE RESIZE IS AN ANISOTROPIC SQUASH. T.Resize((270,270)) is a square target,
    so the 720x1280 ego view is squashed from aspect 1.778 to 1.0. The wrist
    views are 1080x1080 and unaffected. serve_view_preprocess reproduces the same
    squash, so train and serve agree - the squash is the CONTRACT, not a bug, and
    the bridge must hand the ego view at its own native aspect.
  * ROTATION LEAVES BLACK CORNERS. T.RandomRotation defaults to expand=False,
    fill=0. Rotating a 270-px square by 5 deg leaves a largest safe inscribed
    square of 270/(cos5+sin5) = 249.2 px, which is SMALLER than the 256 crop, so
    every rotated training sample keeps a small zero-filled corner wedge (~7 px
    at the extreme). Eval and serving skip rotation entirely and never show black
    corners - a mild train/serve asymmetry, stated here so nobody rediscovers it.
  * THE PER-VIEW SCOPING IS LOAD-BEARING. VideoCrop.get_transform asserts that
    all views it is applied to share one native resolution. This dataset has
    720x1280 and 1080x1080 views, so a SHARED VideoCrop would fail the assert.
    It passes only because each VideoCrop instance is scoped to one key.

Eval/serve mode collapses the whole chain to: resize 270x270 -> centre-crop 256,
per view, no colour change. The switch is `transforms.eval()`, applied per
dataset object (train_starvla.py for the holdout; policy_norm_processor for
serving).

*** SERVING CONSEQUENCE - do not skip this ***
A server that resizes STRAIGHT to 256 shows the model 5% more field of view than
it ever saw. The DataConfig therefore exposes `serve_view_preprocess(images)`:
PIL resize to 270x270 bilinear, then slice [7:263, 7:263]. The server calls it
through
  deployment/model_server/policy_norm_processor.py   -> `data_config` property
  deployment/model_server/gr00t_obs_adapter.py       -> obs_to_example()
After that hook the framework's own obs_image_size resize is a no-op. This is
identical to the round-2 contract and does NOT change for an absolute head.

FIVE CONFIG GOTCHAS, all verified in source:

  1. GRADIENT CHECKPOINTING IS DECLARED BUT NOT ACTIVE.
     Both framework.qwenvl.enable_gradient_checkpointing and
     trainer.gradient_checkpointing are true in the resolved config, and NEITHER
     has a reader on the QwenOFT / Qwen3-VL path. Corroborated by memory: ~86 GB
     resident per GPU at per-device batch 16 on a B200. If you ever need the
     memory, you will have to wire it up - the yaml does not do it.

  2. `min_lr: 1.0e-06` IS NOT THE FLOOR ANY GROUP REACHES.
     transformers converts min_lr to a RATIO against the optimizer's default lr,
     i.e. learning_rate.base:  1e-6 / 2.5e-5 = 0.04. The actual final-step floors
     are therefore  action_model 4.0e-06  and  qwen_vl_interface 4.0e-07. The
     name is misleading whenever a yaml has several per-group LRs.

  3. `learning_rate.base` GOVERNS ZERO PARAMETERS - but is still load-bearing.
     Only two param groups are created (qwen_vl_interface, action_model); the
     'base' group is only added if leftover params exist, and there are none. But
     base IS passed as AdamW's default lr, which is what gotcha 2 divides by.

  4. `attn_implementation: sdpa` IS INERT - the Qwen3-VL path hard-forces sdpa
     regardless, so flash_attention_2 cannot be selected from the yaml at all.

  5. TWO DEAD KEYS. `trainer.weight_decay: 0.0` is dead (the live one is the
     nested trainer.optimizer.weight_decay = 1e-08) and `trainer.max_grad_norm`
     is dead (the live one is trainer.gradient_clipping). Both happen to be
     harmless here, but do not "fix" them expecting an effect.

Also injected by dataclass defaults, not present in the yaml:
  grasp_loss_weight 1.0 / grasp_weight_dim 25 / grasp_weight_thresh -0.143
  -> press-phase weighting DISABLED (1.0 == plain L1); grasp_weight_dim 25 would
  be out of range for a 3-dim action anyway.
  future_action_window_size 29 / past_action_window_size 0 (derived from
  action_horizon 30 by the config-compat normalisation).

*** THE LOGGED `epoch` NUMBER UNDERCOUNTS BY 2x. *** It is
completed_steps / len(per-rank dataloader) and ignores gradient accumulation, so
at step 24000 it will read ~5.0, not ~10.0. The 10.0 above is the true figure.

Why 16 x 2 and not 32 x 1: 32 x 1 fits in memory on an idle B200 (~117 GB), but
round 2 launched while another user held 66 GB on GPU 0. 16 x 2 was kept for
round 3 so the three runs stay byte-comparable.
num_workers 12 (at 4 both round-2 arms were data-bound at ~1.6 s/it),
prefetch_factor 2, persistent_workers, pin_memory.

Observed: 1.18-1.28 s/step on GPUs 4-7 -> ~7.5-8.3 h for 24,000 steps. GPU 5 is
shared with another user's small job; that rate already includes it.

Checkpoints land at steps 5000 / 10000 / 15000 / 20000 as
`checkpoints/steps_<N>_pytorch_model.pt`. 24000 is NOT a multiple of 5000, so
there is no step-24000 checkpoint - the final weights exist only as
`<run_dir>/final_model/pytorch_model.pt` (~9.14 GB).

8. HOLDOUT EVAL PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Opt-in key    datasets.vla_data.eval_data_mix (unset -> no holdout eval at all)
Mixture       unitree_g1_pipette_3view_oft_ee_abs_eval_mix -> the -ee-eval dir.
              A different DIRECTORY from the train mixture, which is what makes
              the disjointness episode-level and structural.
Sampling      Rank r takes the contiguous block [r*K*B, (r+1)*K*B) with
              K = eval_num_batches = 4 and B = eval_batch_size (unset, so it
              silently tracks per_device_batch_size = 16). 4 x 16 x 4 ranks =
              256 fixed samples = 7,680 (sample, step) rows = 23,040 scalars.
              Only the first 256 of the 56,115 eval frames are ever scored.
Determinism   The mixture is built in mode="val", where the sampler's seed is the
              raw index, so index -> (episode, frame) is a pure function,
              independent of epoch and global seed. The batches are decoded ONCE
              at startup and kept in RAM; each evaluation is 4 forward passes per
              rank with no dataloader involved.
              Empirical proof they are the same samples every time: the baseline
              mse_zero_action is bit-identical (0.0008697964696349001) at steps
              0, 500 and 1000.
Views         transforms.eval() - centre crop, no rotation, no colour jitter,
              i.e. exactly the pixels the policy server produces.
Metrics       eval/mse_holdout       MSE over 23,040 scalars, NORMALISED space
              eval/mse_zero_action   the same for the hold-still baseline
              eval/mse_ratio         holdout / baseline. < 1 beats holding still.
                                     THIS IS THE NUMBER.
              eval/rmse_dim{i}       per-dim RMSE x half_span -> METRES
              eval/rmse_dim{i}_zero  the baseline's, same conversion
              All sums are all-reduced (SUM) before the means, so every logged
              number is over all 256 samples on all 4 ranks.

*** THE BASELINE SWITCH - new in round 3 ***
  datasets.vla_data.eval_baseline: hold_first      (default is still "zero")

  "zero"       base = the normalised image of the PHYSICAL zero action, one
               constant vector repeated everywhere.
  "hold_first" base = each sample's OWN first target row, repeated over the
               horizon. Per-sample, and checked BEFORE the zero branch, so it
               works even if dataset_statistics.json is unreadable.

Why physical zero is meaningless here, concretely: the action is the absolute
hand position in the PELVIS frame, so xyz = (0,0,0) is the pelvis origin - about
0.26 m from the mean commanded hand position. Normalised, that point is
[-4.426, +0.154, -1.808], and the first component is driven right off the end of
the +/-2.2 clamp, so under `zero` the "baseline" is not even a consistent
physical point - it is a clamped artefact. In a DELTA space the zero vector
genuinely means "do not move" and is a fair competitor; in an absolute space it
means "teleport the hand into the robot's pelvis".

Metric KEY NAMES are deliberately unchanged across modes so round-2 and round-3
dashboards overlay cleanly. THE CONSEQUENCE IS A TRAP: mse_ratio is NOT
comparable between rounds, because the denominators are different baselines.
Compare the PHYSICAL per-dim RMSE instead.

The round-3 baseline, measured at step 0 and constant thereafter:
   mse_zero_action  8.698e-04
   rmse_dim0_zero   1.8786 mm   (x)
   rmse_dim1_zero   1.1678 mm   (y)
   rmse_dim2_zero   1.3404 mm   (z)
Read that as: freezing the hand at the start of every half-second window leaves
you 1.2-1.9 mm RMS off per row. That is literally how far the commanded hand
travels in half a second on the held-out episodes, and it is the bar.

SANITY CHECK: if hold_first were NOT active, the baseline in this action space
would be order 0.8, not 8.7e-04. A baseline that small is proof the switch took.

TWO SMALL QUIRKS
  * Every eval_interval fires TWICE (two identical 'holdout eval: step500'
    records), because completed_steps only advances on sync_gradients and
    gradient_accumulation_steps = 2. Step 0 fires once. Harmless: ~2 s of GPU and
    a duplicate wandb point with identical eval values.
  * eval_action_model runs immediately before eval_holdout and scores the CURRENT
    TRAINING batch (in-sample `mse_score`). It consumes one extra training batch
    per fire and is NOT comparable to eval/mse_holdout.

12. SERVING CONTRACT  (NOT YET BUILT for the absolute head)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What the round-2 DELTA path does today (VLAPolicyBridge, branch dev/per-phase -
note the ENTIRE EE serving path is still UNCOMMITTED):

   server returns  right_ee_dxyz (30, 3) metres per step. The wire key is the
                   DataConfig action key with the "action." prefix stripped, so
                   round 3 will emit `right_ee_xyz` with ZERO server edits - the
                   server resolves the DataConfig from the checkpoint's data_mix
                   through DATASET_NAMED_MIXTURES -> ROBOT_TYPE_CONFIG_MAP.
   vla_policy_bridge/ee_delta.py (128 lines, class EEDeltaArm):
                   integrates from the LAST COMMANDED pose (never the measured
                   one - 20 mm of droop would be re-applied every chunk), caps
                   each step at STEP_CAP_M = 5 mm, and solves
                   solve_fixed_orientation(max_iterations=8,
                   position_tolerance_m=2e-5, rotation_tolerance_rad=5e-4),
                   seeded from the previous row's solution. Those tolerances are
                   deliberately tighter than the solver defaults (8e-4 m would be
                   a 0.8 mm DEADBAND at a 0.1 mm signal). An unconverged row
                   keeps the best-effort joint-limit-clipped solution and is
                   counted, never raised.
   bridge hooks    bridge.py:1232-1235 in _produce_chunk (after the NaN/Inf chunk
                   gate, before adapter.normalize_chunk, the nav clamp, the arm
                   gain, diagnostics and the catch-up clamp);
                   _ee_delta_to_arm / _ee_kinematics; the synthetic right_ee_xyz
                   state key in _build_obs; and _executed_action_keys, which
                   rewrites the wire key to `right_arm` for the publish tick and
                   _first_step_targets.

WHAT CHANGES FOR ABSOLUTE ROWS:
   - NO integration and no last-command bookkeeping on the ACTION path: each row
     IS the IK target. This deletes the most delicate part of the delta path, and
     the converter gets strictly smaller.
     (The waist half of the q_base overlay is still needed as IK CONTEXT, because
     the chain runs pelvis -> waist(3) -> right arm.)
   - TWO caps are needed, and one of them is new:
       * per-step: clamp the displacement between consecutive rows (keep 5 mm).
       * FIRST-ROW JUMP: clamp the distance from the last commanded pose to row 0.
         In the delta space this was structurally impossible; with absolute
         targets a mis-normalised or out-of-distribution chunk can ask for a large
         teleport on the very first row. THIS IS THE MAIN NEW SAFETY ITEM.
   - _executed_action_keys needs the same `right_ee_xyz -> right_arm` rename -
     one extra branch in the same function. (Its docstring names the bug it
     fixes: without it the publish tick KeyErrors on every tick, because the wire
     key does not exist in a processed chunk.)
   - Orientation is still HELD - this head is translation-only by construction.
   - Downstream joint-space envelopes (max_arm_delta, catch-up clamp, URDF limit
     clipping) are unchanged, so the safety story does not regress.
   - Keep using _q29_of, never q[:29]: the inspire adapter's 41-dim q interleaves
     left_hand at 22:28, so a flat slice feeds hand REGISTERS to the FK as arm
     radians.
